# Promoter Buying

*Last updated: 10 August 2026*

The Promoter Buying page lists open-market purchases by insiders of S&P 500 companies,
read from their public regulatory filings. This page explains what is being read, how the
list is built, and what it cannot tell you. The page itself is
**[here](https://themicrocapinvestor.github.io/smart-investor-tracker/promoter-buying.html)**,
free, and updated every morning.

## Read this part first

**A list of insider purchases is not a recommendation.** Nothing on the page is a
suggestion to buy or sell anything. Insider buying tells you what insiders did with their
own money. It never tells you why, and the why is the part that matters.

**The United States has no "promoter" category.** What exists instead is the Section 16
insider: officers, directors, and holders of ten percent or more of a company's shares.
They must report trades on Form 4 within two business days. That is the closest American
equivalent of promoter buying disclosures Indian readers will know, and it is what this
page reads.

**We count only real purchases.** A buy on this page is a transaction coded P on a Form
4, an open-market purchase with the insider's own money. Option exercises, share grants,
and gifts are excluded by their transaction codes, mechanically. A pre-planned sale or
purchase under a 10b5-1 plan is flagged only in the filing's footnotes, which this page
does not read, so a line here can occasionally be a scheduled trade rather than a fresh
decision.

**What the page does not show matters.** It does not show insider selling, position sizes
relative to the insider's wealth, or anything about companies outside the S&P 500. A
single purchase by a director who owns almost nothing is a different fact from a purchase
by a founder doubling a large holding, and the page does not weight for that.

## How the list is built

The universe is the S&P 500 as published in the publisher's own data export, a fixed
snapshot rather than a live scrape. Each morning, an automated process reads the SEC's
daily filing index, selects every Form 4 filed by an S&P 500 company insider, and keeps
the open-market purchases. The page holds the last thirty one days and a slider widens
the view from one week to the full month. Companies link to StockAnalysis.com pages
carrying our referral code, as disclosed in our [Terms of Service](terms.html).

The process is mechanical: the same filings on the same morning produce the same list.
No company is added or removed by judgement, and no insider is contacted or aware of
their inclusion.

## Sources

The filings are public records published by the **United States Securities and Exchange
Commission** on EDGAR and are free for anyone to read. Every line on the page comes from
a specific Form 4, and anyone can pull the same documents from EDGAR and check our
reading against the originals.
