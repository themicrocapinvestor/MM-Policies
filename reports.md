# Reports

*Last updated: 11 August 2026*

The Reports page collects, in one place, the latest reports from S&P 500
companies: annual reports (Form 10-K, kept for ninety days), quarterly reports
(Form 10-Q, kept for forty five days), results announcements (the earnings
release furnished as an 8-K exhibit under Item 2.02), and investor
presentations that were filed with the SEC (Regulation FD exhibits).
Everything on it is read from public regulatory filings. The page itself is
**[here](https://tracker.themicrocapminute.in/reports.html)**,
free, and updated every morning.

## Read this part first

**There are no upcoming dates on this page, because none are filed.**
American companies are not required to file upcoming result dates or
earnings-call schedules with the SEC, and call recordings and transcripts are
not filed either. This page therefore shows documents that exist, not dates
or audio. Any site that shows upcoming American earnings dates is working
from calendars and estimates, not filings.

**A report is the company's own account of itself.** Management writes it,
and management chooses what to emphasise. Reading it is the start of work,
not the end.

**Nothing here is a recommendation.** Nothing on the page is a suggestion to
buy or sell anything. A new annual report tells you a document was filed. It
never tells you what to do about it.

## How the page is built

The universe is the S&P 500 as published in the publisher's own data export, a
fixed snapshot rather than a live scrape. Each morning, an automated process
searches the SEC's full-text filing search for the forms listed above and
keeps any filing that names an S&P 500 company. The process is mechanical:
the same filings on the same morning produce the same tables. No document is
added or removed by judgement, and no company named on the page is contacted
or aware of its inclusion.

The revenue and profit figures on the annual-reports table come from the SEC's
machine-readable company facts service, known as XBRL. They are the company's
own filed numbers, not our estimates and not a data vendor's.

## Sources

The filings are public records published by the **United States Securities and
Exchange Commission** on EDGAR and are free for anyone to read. The tables are
built with the SEC's full-text search and its company facts API, both free for
anyone to use. Anyone can pull the same documents and figures from the SEC
and check our reading against the originals.

The SEC is not affiliated with us in any way, and nothing here is published
with its knowledge or approval. No company named on the page has any
connection to this publication. The SEC's own rules on reusing what it
publishes, and how we follow them, are set out in
[SEC Data and Public Filings](sec-data.html).
